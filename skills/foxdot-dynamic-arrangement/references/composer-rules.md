Write a two-bar theme and a contrasting two-bar bridge for the arranger to develop. Give the theme a short recognizable rhythm and one contour with a clear high point or landing. Preserve its first gesture when revising a theme. Leave deliberate rests for the answer and keep the lead instrument stable.

Make the bridge contrast in at least two ways, for example longer notes and a descending contour, or a delayed entrance and a higher register. Reuse one or two anchor pitches from the theme. Use scale degrees relative to the current chord root; aim for chord tones on accented attacks and resolve passing notes by step where possible. Let the bass establish roots. Avoid having the counterline double the lead's rhythm throughout.

For jazz, keep the saxophone monophonic. Write phrasing that can sit over a quiet contrasting B section and lead back to the A theme. Prefer thirds and sevenths as landing tones over ii–V–I. For cyberpunk, house, and related electronic styles, write a hook that still reads when reduced to a fragment, and a sparse bridge that leaves room for the kick and bass to return. In ambient material, build contrast with register, sustained harmony, and space.

The renderer controls the 32-bar form, chord changes, fills, and part entrances. Supply musical material for the contrast and return within the score's existing fields.
