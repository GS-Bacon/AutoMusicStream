# Why the form changes

| Dimension | What to listen for | Use in FoxDot Radio |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Phrase function | A short opening gesture establishes what can later be varied. Shortening or interrupting the answer creates forward motion. | The intro previews two attacks per bar. Theme and return share their pitch and rhythm material. The return gives part of the theme a higher register and the ending holds a tonic. |
| Melodic contour and space | A repeated contour can remain recognizable with different note lengths or a delayed entrance. Rests expose the answer and make the next attack matter. | The bridge has a separate score field. Older scores derive it from the theme's first and last pitches. Each preset supplies its own bridge. |
| Harmonic rhythm and cadence | The speed of chord changes is audible independently of tempo. Faster changes can signal a transition. A stable harmony after that motion can signal arrival. | Half-bar changes in the approach to the return or ending lead into a held tonic. Jazz ends with ii–V–I. The electronic preset can use modal movement from the seventh degree back to tonic. |
| Jazz form and voicing | A–A′–B–A″ recalls the opening idea after a contrasting bridge. Thirds and sevenths describe chord quality; nearby inversions make the progression smoother. | Jazz uses four eight-bar sections with a contrasting bridge, walking bass, and accompaniment voiced around thirds and sevenths. |
| Electronic tension and release | Subtract bass and kick, increase activity on selected upper parts, leave a brief gap, then restore the low end. A change in texture can mark arrival without a tempo change. | Cyberpunk and the dance styles use intro, theme, breakdown, build, climax, and outro. The build opens the lead filter and adds fills. Ambient keeps the contrast in the sustained layers and skips the drum fill. |
| Arrangement and dynamics | Compare several bars at equal monitoring gain. Listen for differences in groove, instrument roles, register, and note density. | Section levels are applied to individual parts. The band remains consistent. A 32-bar render makes the full trajectory available for audition. |

These are arrangement choices for this generator. They apply the sources' concepts to these songs and do not prescribe a single correct form for a genre.

- Phrase presentation, continuation, and cadence. [Open Music Theory, The Phrase, Archetypes, and Unique Forms](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheorycopy/chapter/phrase-archetypes-unique-forms/) and [Introduction to Harmony](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheorycopy/chapter/intro-to-harmony/).
- Jazz form, harmonic function, and voice leading. [AABA Form and Strophic Form](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory/chapter/aaba-and-strophic-form/), [ii–V–I](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheorycopy/chapter/ii-v-i/), and [Jazz Voicings](https://viva.pressbooks.pub/openmusictheory/chapter/jazz-voicings/).
- Electronic form and changes in sound over time. [Bradley Osborn, Formal Functions and Rotations in Top-40 EDM](https://theory.esm.rochester.edu/integral/36-2023/osborn/) and [Jeremy W. Smith, The Functions of Continuous Processes in Contemporary Electronic Dance Music](https://mtosmt.org/issues/mto.21.27.2/mto.21.27.2.smith.html).
- Production and motif development. [Dennis DeSantis, Dramatic Arc](https://makingmusic.ableton.com/dramatic-arc), [Arranging as a Subtractive Process](https://makingmusic.ableton.com/arranging-as-a-subtractive-process), [Creating Melodies 1](https://makingmusic.ableton.com/creating-melodies-1-contour), and [Unique Events](https://makingmusic.ableton.com/unique-events).
